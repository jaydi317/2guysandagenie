The Event Email Series: How It Works

What Daegan and Claude designed on the night of Nov 9 to 10, for everyone who registers for the next free night. Status: the skill page is a mock-up with all 12 days drafted (v5: two new opening days, three skills taken out); the emails are not rewritten yet.

The Job

Everyone who registers shows up excited, ready, and bringing a friend. The series is also a reason to register: "You'll know more about AI than you ever thought you could, before you even walk in."

The Layers, In Order

  1. A story in each email (Jamon, Maestro's, Dr. McCray, Jim's bread, Bruce, Ian and more), told the Story Selling way: Batman openings, open loops, "previous episodes."
  2. A visual wherever one carries the point: doodles, before-and-afters, quotes, stats.
  3. A five-minute skill each day on the reader's own page, Your Seat. Your Skills. Each unlocks after the one before.
  4. A baseline, never the answer. Each skill leaves them holding something (a number, a photo, three ideas). "We're getting ready, not making changes yet."
  5. An open loop the night closes, pointed at with a different note each day.
  6. "What did you learn?" after each skill. With their OK, their words become testimonials on that skill.
  7. "Who do you know that would love this?" A share step: text, email, Facebook, copy the link.

The Notes That Point At The Night

Vary them. Never the same note twice in a row. Never withholding, constraining or commanding.

  • Direct: "On the night, a few names go up on the big screen."
  • Why we do it together: "Finding the one line that belongs at the top is the part we do together."
  • Someone else's story: "I think with AI by itself... without you, I think it would have failed." (Jamon)
  • Love of the work: "One of my favorite moments, every time we do this with a group."
  • Curiosity: "I'm curious what we'll come up with once we start playing around with it."

The 12 Skills

  1. Just ask it something (feet wet: voice, anything you're curious about, a few times)
  2. The to-do list you'll use for the rest of your life (Daegan's own system, given away whole)
  3. Hand AI the job you dread
  4. See your business through a stranger's eyes (with Jamon's video)
  5. Count the customers your phone lost
  6. See your website the way a stranger does
  7. Test your business name
  8. Write the thank-you that brings them back
  9. Run the number nobody ran for you
  10. Ask AI who's the best near you
  11. Get a read on anyone
  12. Have AI read your inbox (ends with: need things kept confidential? Remind us in the room)

Taken out on Nov 10, and why. Watch AIs build your website (Design Arena): it devalues the mock-up we make for them in the room ("I could do that on my own"). Lock down your AI: now something they remind us to cover in the room. The NotebookLM video: finished work they can run off with alone.

The rule for what stays. Lose the skills that let someone run off and do finished business work alone. Keep the open-ended ones, where they're holding something they don't know what to do with yet and the room makes something of it. A skill that only makes their life easier (the to-do list, the inbox) is fine to give away whole.
