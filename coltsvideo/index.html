A real job · Filmed Sunday, Nov 8 · Edited by AI

Three Minutes Of Phone Video. One Message To AI. Here's What Came Back.

Sunday I was standing above a stadium tunnel with my iPhone while the players ran out.

My chiropractor of many years, Dr. Marcus McCray, walked out with them. He's the Team Chiropractor, Indianapolis Colts.

Somewhere in 3 minutes and 15 seconds of shaky video, he looks up, smiles and throws me a peace sign.

It lasts half a second.

Here's what AI made out of it.

The half second, slowed down. Ready for Reels and Stories.

What I Asked For

One message to Claude. In plain English. Roughly:

"Find today's game footage. Cut one video of the team coming out of the tunnel and one of Doc walking out, he smiles and throws a peace sign. Make phone and widescreen versions. Pull the best photos. Put it all in a dated folder and make me a page to review it."

No editing software opened by me. No timeline dragged. No settings picked.

What Came Back, In Under An Hour

6finished clips, phone and widescreen
16photos pulled straight from the video
4timelines in a real pro editing project

Plus a review page, every file named and dated, and a note in our project notes saying where it all lives.

The Walk-Out

Widescreen, real speed. 7 seconds.

The Team Coming Out

The 7 best moments out of 3 minutes, cut into 14 seconds.

The Phone Versions

Same footage, turned tall. The picture slides along with the players so nobody runs out of frame.

The Photos Nobody Had To Take

It went through the video frame by frame and kept the sharpest one at each big moment.

Every one of these came out of the video. Nobody pressed the shutter.

The Part That Surprised Me

  1. It noticed my two videos were really one. The 18-second clip I'd texted myself was just the last 18 seconds of the big file. So it cut everything from the full-quality original instead.
  2. It found the half second on its own. Doc smiles at 2:59.1. The peace sign runs from 2:59.25 to 2:59.75. It measured that, it didn't guess.
  3. It caught a color problem and fixed it. iPhone video looked washed out inside the editing software. It compared a frame against Apple's own version of the same frame and matched it.
  4. It saved my other project first. I had a different video open. It saved it, did this job, and put mine back exactly where I left it.

That's the difference between asking AI a question and handing it a job.
