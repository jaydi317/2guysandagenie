Einstein In The Basement

A genius downstairs, a chimp with a rock, and why "behind" is the wrong word for where you are.

By Jimmy Davis · 5 minute read

ROUGH DRAFT. This is an early version we are still working on. Pictures and wording will change. Tell us what lands and what loses you: hello@2guysandagenie.com

Imagine Albert Einstein lives in your basement.

He's down there right now. Smartest guy who ever lived. Wild hair, little desk, right next to the water heater.

And the only thing you ever ask him to do is proofread your kid's book report.

That's how most people are using AI right now.

I'm barely exaggerating.

Most People Use AI For Recipes

Ask around. Ask your neighbor, your cousin, the guy next to you at the chamber lunch. What do you use ChatGPT for?

Most people are using it for recipes. "What can I make with chicken and broccoli?"

Are you kidding me?

Hold on to that picture. We're coming back down those stairs at the end.

Where Everybody Actually Is

Maybe you've been thinking, "I'm so far behind on this AI stuff."

Look at this first. This is where most people really are.

Most people have never really used AI.

A lot have tried it once or twice.

A small group uses it every day.

And a tiny sliver are what you'd call power users.

From what I see, most small business owners have at least tried it. Only a small slice have it actually working in their business.

So when you picture "everybody else" racing ahead of you? They're not. Look at the grid again. Most of the boxes are empty.

Most of the world is asleep on this. And the ones who are awake mostly use it like a search box.

Here's something I can't stop thinking about. I'm losing sleep over it, in a good way. The whole world is asleep, and I'm the one up at 1 a.m.

Why Everybody Stopped Looking

So why is everyone asleep? It's not because people are lazy or dumb.

It's first impressions.

Most people tried ChatGPT back in 2023. They typed a question, got a decent answer, and decided, "Oh. It's a chatbot." Then they never looked again.

It's like judging a restaurant forever by one cold bowl of soup on opening night. Three years later it might be the best place in town, and you're still telling people about the soup.

They're still using it like it's 2023.

Meanwhile the thing kept growing. Remember No. 1? It read the tiny print on my Colts ticket and told me which door to use. Remember No. 2? Every book on every shelf, squeezed down and put in your phone. That's not a chatbot. That's a genius who's been waiting downstairs for someone to ask him something hard.

Imagine You Found The Craft

Now let's get a little weird. Just imagine this one with me.

Imagine somebody, somewhere, found an alien craft. A real one. And nobody came with it. No pilot, no instructions.

So they just keep trying things to get it to work. Pull this. Push that. Bang on the hatch with a wrench. Squint at a page of notes nobody can read.

That would be like a chimp using a rock to try and get an iPhone to work.

That's where AI is right now, from where I sit. The most powerful thing ever built, and most of us are banging on it with a rock.

And I'm not pointing at you. That was me, a year ago. Rock in hand.

The Recipe And The Chef

Let's go back to the kitchen for a second, because there's one more step here.

Using AI like a chatbot is like looking up a recipe and cooking every step yourself.

You ask. It answers. Then you do all the work: chop, stir, burn the garlic, wash the pans.

Here's what that looks like in a shop. You ask ChatGPT to write a follow-up email to a customer who got an estimate. It writes a nice one. Then you copy it, paste it, dig up their email address, send it, and try to remember to check back Thursday.

Helpful? Sure. But you're still the cook.

What's coming is calling the chef, sitting down, and waiting for the plate.

The chef version sounds like this: "Follow up with everybody who got an estimate this week." And it's handled, by a helper that does the whole job and checks with you before anything goes out.

That's not science fiction. It's already starting. (How a chatbot gets "hands" is a whole story of its own. It's No. 8.)

The Small Group's Secret

Then there's a small group. They figured out it does things now. You can just talk to it, and it builds things.

So what do they know that everybody else doesn't?

Their secret? No manual. They just play with it. Every day. (Remember the bike from No. 1.)

The people who win aren't the smartest ones. They're the ones who play with it the most.

That's honestly our whole edge at 2 Guys and a Genie. Paul and I aren't geniuses. We just play with it all day, every day, so the people we work with don't have to start from zero.

You're Not Behind. You're Early.

Here's why I think this is the best news a small business owner has heard in years.

Think about your town. Fishers, Noblesville, Carmel, wherever you are. How many plumbers? How many chiropractors? How many insurance agents?

If the grid is anywhere close to right, most of them are asleep. Their Einstein is still proofreading book reports.

You're not behind. You're early.

And early is the best place to be in a small town market. You don't have to beat Amazon. You just have to be the first one on your street who wakes up.

It won't stay this way. Every month these tools get easier, and more people wake up. That's not a threat. That's a head start, and it's yours if you take it.

And you don't need tech skills, money, or a team to start. You need to be curious enough to walk downstairs.

Steal This

Ask Einstein a real question (five minutes, any AI)

Tonight, skip the recipe. Open ChatGPT or Claude, tap the microphone, and say this out loud:

"Here's my business: [what you do, where, and who your customers are]. Here's the thing I'm stuck on this week: [the call you keep putting off, the slow month, the job you hate]. Before you answer, ask me three questions. Then think it through step by step and give me a plan I could start tomorrow morning."

Then answer its questions like you're talking to a smart friend.

Go Downstairs

So here's the picture again.

Einstein's still in your basement. Same desk. Same water heater. He hasn't gone anywhere. He's been waiting.

This time, leave the book report upstairs.

Bring him the thing that keeps you up at night, and watch what he does with it.
