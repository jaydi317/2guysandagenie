Step 1 For Every Shop

Slydial First (Jimmy)

Slydial drops your voicemail without ringing their phone. Record each one live, so it sounds like you. Then call or walk in the next day.

Hey [owner's name], this is Jimmy.

I tried to catch you and missed you, so I thought I'd leave you a quick message. Don't worry, I'll try you again [tomorrow].

I'm one of the new local AI guys here in [Carmel], and I'm going around introducing myself to the businesses around here.

The way I like to do that is to show you what I can do.

Pick one thing in your business you'd love solved, and I'll come back and show you how.

That way, next time something like that comes up, you'll know who to call.

It's Jimmy, [Jimmy's number]. Talk soon.

How to send a Slydial (2 minutes)
  1. Get it once: the slydial app (iPhone or Android), or just dial 267-759-3425 from any phone. U.S. numbers only.
  2. Open the shop in Leads. Copy their number and the owner's first name.
  3. Enter their number in slydial. It goes straight to their voicemail.
  4. Say the message above, live, with their name and yours. About 30 seconds.
  5. Mark the shop "Visited" in Leads and add a note: "Slydial sent [date]."
  6. Next day: call them (Phone Call script) or walk in (Walk-In script). Book the 15 minutes.

Premium ($2.99 a month) skips the ads. Record each one live rather than one recording sent to many: it sounds like you, and a pre-recorded message dropped on a cell phone can be treated like a robocall. If a shop's number rings through instead of going to voicemail, it's likely a landline: just leave the message live.

After You Look Them Up

The One-Thing Demo

After an audit, or the second time you talk to them. Show one thing they can see, tell them how it came out for a client, and ask to sit down. Don't send them the report: to a busy owner it's a pile they'll get to "someday." Don't knock whoever built their site. Name the other things you found, never how to fix them. Get your three things from "Run The Audit" on the shop's card in Leads.

1. Show them one thing.

I spent some time looking at your business online, and I found something you can see right now. Pull up [your website on your phone].

[The one thing, in plain words. Example: "On the first screen, there's no button to call you."]

[Let them look. Seeing it themselves is what makes it real.]

2. How it came out for a client.

Jimmy went to work on the business of a handyman in Indy named Jamon. Jamon went from about $15,000 a month to over $50,000. He got so many customers he had to pause his ads while he built what he needed to keep up.

We can do the same thing with you.

3. That's just the beginning.

And [that one thing] is only the first thing we found. There are several more that could make a real difference.

[Name one more. One line. No how.]

[Name another. One line. No how.]

Only if the audit gives a money read:

All told, we found [number] things. By our read from the outside, fixing them could mean around [the audit's conservative figure] more a year. Your real numbers would tell us for sure.

So it's worth 15 minutes to sit down and really look.

4. The sit-down.

I really think there's something here we can do together. Something you might get really excited about. But we won't know until we sit down and look at it together. Can we find time this week, you, me and Jimmy?

If they ask how to fix it:

There are a few ways, and the right one depends on how your business runs. That's what the sit-down is for.

After They Say Yes

The 15 Minutes: What To Ask

Questions only. No fix, no advice on this call. Put 20 minutes on the calendar; it runs over.

Open: "Thanks for the time. I'm just going to ask a few questions, then we'll go do our homework."

  1. If we fixed one thing in the next 30 days, which would matter most: more money, more hours back, or happier customers?
  2. What do you or your team do over and over every week?
  3. Where do things slow down, get stuck, or fall through the cracks?
  4. About how many hours a week does that take, and who does it?
  5. If that one thing ran itself, what would change for you?

The follow-up to almost anything: "Walk me through how that works today."

Say it back: "So the one thing is [their bottleneck], and it's costing you about [hours] a week. Did I get that right?"

Book the next one before you hang up: "Give us a few days. When can we meet again so I can show you what we found?"

Only If They Ask

"What Does It Cost?"

Never bring it up yourself. Only when they ask, on the walk-in, the phone, or the 15 minutes.

"There's no cost to you.

You tell us the one thing, and we go find what fixes it.

If it's a tool that's already out there, we'll tell you what it costs and show you how to use it, the way we've set it up for our clients.

If it's something we'd build for you, we'll tell you that too, and you decide if you want it.

Really, this is just us showing you what we do. So you get a feel for who we are and how we can help.

Then next time something comes up, or you think, "I bet AI could help with this," you'll know who to call.

That's the whole point. Fair?"
