Who We Looked For

Daegan's words: "the type of businesses that are willing to spend money. They're active, they're excited, they're looking forward to get better and grow. Not just the ones that need the most help."

Everything is inside 30 miles of Launch Fishers, 12175 Visionary Way.

Every business gets two questions.

  1. Is this owner worth it? Yes, if he spends to grow (ads, paid tools, an agency, hiring). Also yes, if he is loved and he is the business (strong reviews, the owner named in them). Jimmy's own clients look like the second kind far more often than the first.
  2. Is there a leak costing him money today that we can show him? Seen in a review or on his own site. A phone that goes to voicemail, a call never returned, hours on the site that differ from Google, an online store locked behind a password.

How A Round Works

A round covers about seven areas. Each round changed the method for the next.

  1. Sweep by place, not by trade. Around each town centre, ten broad Google searches that name no trade ("family owned business", "locally owned shop", "repair shop", and so on). The first try searched by ten trades and got ten trades back. Searching the place returned dozens of trades.
  2. Fit check. Out go schools, churches, government, hospitals, banks, national chains, and anything past 30 miles or already found. A locally owned franchise stays, on the lowest rung.
  3. Read the reviews. For each listing: the rating, how many reviews, whether customers name the owner, and whether a customer describes a leak in their own words.
  4. Find the site and check it is theirs. A lookup sometimes returns a different company with the same name, once in another state.
  5. Read the site as a customer. Can I reach them, book, see prices, see proof? Is anything stale or broken? Do the hours and phone match the Google listing?
  6. Grade. A, B, worth a visit or franchise, by the table above.
  7. Run Jimmy's clients through the same two questions. If the questions would have missed them, the questions are wrong. The first version would have missed most of them, which is why "loved and he is the business" became a way to qualify.
  8. Save what the round taught, so the next round is better.

It is research on the public face of each business only: its site, its listing, its reviews.

What The Grades Can And Cannot Tell You

  • An A is evidence, not a promise. The strongest A's have a customer saying in their own words that a sale was lost. The weaker ones rest on hours that differ between the site and Google, which could be a stale listing.
  • The B's are not all the same depth. Some got a full customer read of the site. Others got a lighter check: the site is theirs, whether the phone and hours are on it, and whether the year at the bottom of the site is old.
  • "Owner named in reviews" comes from the five reviews Google shows, not every review the shop has.
  • Everything was seen on November 16, 2026. Hours, phones and sites change. Recheck a shop the morning you call it or walk in.
  • No owner's private life was looked at.

Not done yet: the deeper check on the A's (their Facebook, Instagram and Yelp, and how they compare to the leading shop in their trade and town), and owner names for most rows.
