# Report 2: The Business Joiner
For: Jessica Dworzak, independent rep with **PlanNet Marketing** (sells the InteleTravel home-based travel agency). Fast pass, 2026-11-09. GUESS = not verified.

## 1. What a joiner actually joins
Two layers, and joiners often confuse them:
- **InteleTravel Independent Travel Agency (ITA):** you become a home-based travel advisor and earn commission on trips you book. A 2025 review site lists the fee as **$179.95 to start plus $39.95/month** (verify current price). Source: https://www.plannetmarketing.com/en-us/Your-Product (via search summary) and agent reviews.
- **PlanNet Marketing Rep:** optional layer for people who want to build a team. **$19.95 to start, $19.95/month**, per PlanNet's 2024 income disclosure. Source: https://www.plannetmarketing.com/en-us/Income-Disclosure
- In the US you enroll through a PlanNet rep even if you only want to be a travel agent. Source: https://hostagencyreviews.com/blog/travel-mlms
- PlanNet promotes a "Profit Guarantee" and a free personalized website (company claim, terms not checked). Source: https://www.plannetmarketing.com/en-us/Your-Product

## 2. The hard numbers she must respect
From PlanNet's own Income Disclosure Statement:
- **2025:** 16.04% of reps earned any commission or override; **83.96% earned nothing.** The entry "Rep" rank (92.52% of members) averaged **$38.15 for the year.** Source: https://hostagencyreviews.com/blog/travel-mlms
- **2024:** Rep rank averaged $40.27; Bronze Builders $780.31, Silver $2,187.57, Gold $5,083.26 (averages, before expenses). Source: https://www.plannetmarketing.com/en-us/Income-Disclosure and PlanNet compliance blog https://www.plannetnow.com/compliance-corner-100
- Fees alone at the Rep level are about $259 a year ($19.95 x 13), which is more than the average Rep earns. Joiners who do the math will notice.

**What this means:** Her honest pitch is "learn the travel advisor business and get travel perks," not "make money." Any income talk will hurt her credibility and break FTC rules.

## 3. FTC income-claims line (read this before writing any copy)
From FTC "Business Guidance Concerning Multi-Level Marketing" (updated 2024): https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing
- Any earnings claim must reflect what a **typical** person is likely to earn, **after expenses**.
- Showing top earners or atypical results is likely deceptive.
- **Lifestyle claims count**: "quit my job," "fire your boss," "paid to travel the world," car or mansion photos.
- If you don't know typical earnings, make **no** earnings claim.
- The FTC is actively enforcing this against individual top reps on social media (April 2026 proposed order; June 2026 FTC blog). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/06/back-those-earnings-claims-other-lessons-ftcs-labor-task-force-work

**Safe:** "Learn how travel advisors work." "Training and a team to help you." "Travel perks for advisors" (if real). "Here are the costs, and here is the company's income disclosure."
**Not safe:** "Earn $X a month." "Replace your income." "Get paid to travel." "Retire your husband." Screenshots of checks. Any number without the IDS context.

## 4. Who joins (GUESS, from industry pattern and reviews)
- **Moms**, often stay-at-home or part-time, wanting flexible work. Over 75% of the MLM workforce is women. Source: https://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/i-got-rich-working-for-an-mlm-and-it-cost-me-everything-that-truly-mattered_uk_64b93cd1e4b038c60cc91828
- **Travel lovers** who already plan trips for friends and family.
- **Side-hustlers** with a day job.
- **Near-retirees and retirees** who want something fun and social.
- **Ex-MLM people** who are wary but still drawn to the travel angle.

## 5. What they want
- Travel and travel perks (agent rates, familiarization trips). GUESS on which perks InteleTravel currently offers; verify.
- Flexibility: work from home, around kids.
- Community and belonging. Reviews say: "It all depends on who trains you and which team you are in" and "choose your team wisely!" Sources: https://www.indeed.com/cmp/Inteletravel/reviews , https://www.trustpilot.com/review/inteletravel.com
- Some extra income (never promise it).
- A skill and identity: "I'm a travel advisor."

## 6. Fears and objections
| Fear | Their likely words | Honest response |
|---|---|---|
| Pyramid stigma | "Isn't this a pyramid scheme?" | "Here's how it works and what it costs. The team part is optional. Here's the company's own income disclosure." Transparency beats defensiveness. |
| Pestering friends | "I don't want to be that person on Facebook." | Teach a no-pressure approach: helping people plan trips they were already taking. |
| Failing again | "I lost money in my last MLM." | Show real costs up front and say most people earn little. Oddly, honesty is the differentiator here. |
| Fees add up | "You work for free until the customer returns from the trip." (Glassdoor agent review) | Explain commission timing: often 30 to 60 days after travel per agent reviews. https://www.glassdoor.com/Reviews/Inteletravel-com-Independent-Travel-Agent-Reviews-EI_IE806544.0,16_KO17,41.htm |
| Weak tools/support | "The agent website provided to me was not fit for purpose." (Trustpilot UK) | Show what training and support Jessica's team gives, specifically. |
| Personal travel only | "If you're only using it for personal holidays it may well not be worth it." (Trustpilot) | Agree. Tell people who it's NOT for. |

## 7. What makes top recruiters credible (pattern, GUESS from reviewed agent sites)
- They lead with the **travel business**, not the money (e.g. agent sites like https://www.catinathebroker.com/blog/travelagentwithinteletravel list training, flexibility, deals and perks).
- Real photos of their own trips and clients, not stock luxury.
- Clear costs on the page.
- "Who this is for / not for."
- A small, specific first step (coffee chat, info video) instead of "DM me."
- Independent reviewers flag reps sending a personal sign-up link as "a huge red flag" (https://hostagencyreviews.com/blog/travel-mlms). So make the first step a conversation, not a checkout link.

## 8. Five headline ideas (all compliant: no income claims)
1. Love planning trips? Learn how travel advisors book them from home.
2. A home-based travel business, explained honestly. Real costs. No hype.
3. Curious about becoming a travel advisor? Start with a 15-minute coffee chat, not a pitch.
4. Learn the travel business with a team that trains you, not pressures you.
5. Already the friend everyone asks for trip advice? Here's how advisors do it.

## 9. Recommended page structure for joiners
1. Headline + her photo + one-line story.
2. What a travel advisor does (3 bullets).
3. What it costs (exact current numbers) and a link to the official income disclosure.
4. What training and support you get from her team.
5. Who it's for / who it's not for.
6. Book a 15-minute coffee chat button.
7. Footer disclaimer: "Results vary. Most participants earn little or no money. See the PlanNet Income Disclosure Statement."
