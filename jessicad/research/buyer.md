# Report 1: The Vacation Buyer
For: Jessica Dworak, independent rep with **PlanNet Marketing** (correct spelling: capital P, capital N; "Planet Marketing" was a transcription error).
Researched 2026-11-09, fast pass (about 10 minutes). Items marked GUESS are not verified.

## 1. First, what is she actually selling to a vacation buyer?
This matters more than anything else in this report.

- PlanNet Marketing does **not** sell a US discount travel club. It is the network-marketing arm that sells the **InteleTravel** home-based Independent Travel Agency (ITA). Source: https://hostagencyreviews.com/blog/travel-mlms
- So in the US, a "vacation buyer" for Jessica is **someone who books a trip through her as an InteleTravel travel advisor**, usually through her personalized InteleTravel booking website or by asking her to plan it. PlanNet's own product page says agents book "cruises, vacations, hotels, all-inclusive resorts, tours, airline tickets, car rentals and more" and agents and customers can book through the agent's personal travel website. Source: https://www.plannetmarketing.com/en-us/Your-Product (summarized via search; page blocks direct fetch)
- InteleTravel claims a booking platform with **110+ suppliers** and about **145,000 advisors** worldwide (July 2026). Sources: https://hostagencyreviews.com/blog/travel-mlms , https://www.trustpilot.com/review/inteletravel.com
- **UK exception:** in the UK, PlanNet sells a paid travel membership called the "Society of Intelligent Travelers." Source: https://behindmlm.com/mlm-reviews/plannet-marketing-review-inteletravel-agent-subscriptions/ . If Jessica is US-based, ignore this.
- **What "savings" can she honestly claim?** Travel advisors are paid by supplier commissions, so the trip usually costs the client the same as booking direct (AAA: "trips booked with travel agents typically do not cost more"). Source: https://www.aaa.com/tripcanvas/article/are-travel-agents-worth-it-when-to-self-book-or-hire-a-pro-CM1705 . Do not promise "wholesale" or "up to 70% off" unless InteleTravel's supplier rates actually show it. GUESS: her real edge is service, perks and time saved, not a cheaper price on every trip.

**Recommendation:** Position Jessica as **a personal travel advisor who plans and books your trip for no extra cost**, not as a "travel club" or "discount membership." That avoids the scam-club baggage and the MLM baggage in one move.

## 2. Who buys
GUESSES based on the product and advisor-industry sources, not hard data:
- Busy families planning Disney, cruises or all-inclusives who hate planning.
- Couples: honeymoons, anniversaries, destination weddings.
- Group organizers: girls' trips, family reunions, church or club groups.
- Retirees and first-time cruisers who want a human to call.
- Friends and neighbors of Jessica (warm market) who trust her.

## 3. Their real words, desires and fears
Few public reviews come from InteleTravel customers (most reviews are from agents). Themes below come from advisor-vs-DIY articles and consumer-protection reporting.

**Desires**
- "I just want someone to handle it." Hours of tabs and reviews is the pain.
- Perks: room upgrades, welcome gifts, early check-in, resort credits through advisor partner programs. Source: https://mountainsandmahals.com/travel-agent-vs-diy-trip-planning/ , https://foresttravel.com/journal/is-a-luxury-travel-advisor-worth-it/
- A real person when things go wrong: "When you book online, you're typically one of thousands in a customer service queue." Source: https://zestyears.com/what-travel-agents-get-you-for-free-that-booking-sites-never-will/
- Price drops caught after booking ("Sometimes clients receive price reductions months after booking"). Source: https://verivacation.com/travel/travelInspiration/travel-agent-vs-online-booking.aspx

**Fears**
- Overpaying, or missing a better deal.
- Getting scammed by a "travel club." This fear is earned: the BBB logged thousands of travel club complaints, one man paid $3,475 for a membership and then found the club's Orlando condo price $300 higher than booking himself, and an Illinois case found a club secretly booking through Expedia and Priceline. Source: https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna24506778 , https://www.consumerreports.org/cro/2012/07/vacation-club-scams/index.htm
- Their money going missing. A Sept 2026 BBB complaint says an InteleTravel agent misappropriated over $40,000 in customer funds. Source: https://www.bbb.org/us/fl/delray-beach/profile/travel-agency/inteletravelcom-0633-4009788/customer-reviews . This is one complaint, but buyers will search for it.
- Being "sold to" by a friend in an MLM.

## 4. Objections and how to answer them
| Objection | Honest answer |
|---|---|
| "Is this a scam / an MLM thing?" | "You're not joining anything. You're just booking a trip. Your payment goes to the cruise line, resort or tour company, and I'm paid by them." (Confirm how InteleTravel payments flow before saying this.) |
| "Can't I just use Expedia?" | "You can, and for a quick flight you probably should. For a cruise, resort, Disney or group trip, I cost you nothing extra, I watch for price drops and perks, and you have one person to call." AAA's rule: use an advisor when the cost of a mistake is bigger than the planning time. |
| "Will it cost more?" | Usually no, advisors are paid by suppliers. Source: AAA link above. |
| "What if something goes wrong?" | Show her phone number, response time, and a real story of a rescue (only a real one). |
| "Are you a real travel agent?" | Show her InteleTravel affiliation, any training or certifications she actually holds, and real reviews. |

## 5. Best angles and hooks
1. **Time back.** "Stop spending your vacation planning your vacation."
2. **A human, not a hold line.** Someone who answers the phone when the flight is cancelled.
3. **Second opinion.** "Before you click buy on that cruise, send me the link." Low-risk first step.
4. **Same price, more care.** No extra cost because suppliers pay her (verify per supplier).
5. **Local and personal.** Her face, her story, her own trips. People trust a neighbor more than a website.

Avoid: "travel club," "wholesale," "secret prices," "up to 70% off," "get paid to travel." Those phrases match the scam pattern buyers fear.

## 6. Five headline ideas
1. Plan less. Travel more. I do the booking, you do the beach.
2. Stop spending your vacation planning your vacation.
3. Booking a cruise or resort? Get a free second opinion before you click buy.
4. Same trip. Real human. One person to call if anything goes wrong.
5. Tell me your dream trip. I'll send back three options this week.

## 7. What to show on her site for buyers
- Her photo, short story and the kinds of trips she loves.
- "Request a free trip quote" form (who, where, when, budget).
- Trip types: cruises, all-inclusive, Disney, honeymoons, groups.
- Real reviews and real client trip photos (only real ones).
- "How I'm paid" in one sentence (builds trust).
- Booking site link for people who want to book themselves.
