# Competitor Scan: Travel MLMs and Similar
Fast pass, 2026-11-09. Most cost figures come from review sites and may be years old; verify before quoting. Headlines marked "positioning" are summarized from company or affiliate copy, not verified homepage text (several company sites block automated fetch).

## Company by company

### PlanNet Marketing + InteleTravel (Jessica's company)
- **Positioning:** turn a passion for travel into a flexible home-based travel agency; PlanNet reps "market and sell the InteleTravel Product to individuals who want to turn a passion for travel into a flexible source of income and reward." Source: https://www.plannetmarketing.com/en-us/Your-Business (via search summary)
- **Features promoted:** personalized booking website, 110+ suppliers (cruises, resorts, tours, air, cars), training, "Profit Guarantee," team-building optional.
- **Hook:** become a travel agent from home.
- **Weak spot:** 83.96% of reps earned nothing in 2025 (company IDS). https://hostagencyreviews.com/blog/travel-mlms

### InCruises
- **Positioning:** cruise membership club. Pay monthly, get double in cruise credit ("2-for-1"; credits now called Rewards Points in the member agreement). Source: https://behindmlm.com/mlm-reviews/incruises-review-membership-access-to-cruise-discounts/ , https://files.incruises.com/files/en/106EN_3.2_MEMBER_AGREEMENT.pdf
- **Features:** $100/month traditionally (a $50 Starter plan exists), credits toward cruises, "invitation only" feel, Partner tier for the business ($195 then $95/yr).
- **Hook:** "Every $100 becomes $200 toward a cruise."
- **Scale:** largest travel MLM, about $350M revenue in 2025 (Business For Home figure via https://flawlessmlm.com/en/blog/travel-mlm-companies-guide ).

### Surge365
- **Positioning:** "travel technology company" that says it competes with Expedia, Travelocity and Booking.com. Source: https://corporate.surge365.com/View/Home
- **Features:** customer booking engine for your website, password-protected discount engine, savings club with wholesale travel plus shopping and dining discounts, Vortex hotel savings, Reward Credits / Vacation Vault, 150% price match (affiliate claim), $1,000 Fast Start Bonus (business side).
- **Hook:** "Wholesale travel prices, up to 70% off" (affiliate claim, unverified). https://goteamget.com/surge365-membership
- **Costs:** older source says $495 start plus $99/month. 70.35% of members made no income in 2020. https://flawlessmlm.com/en/blog/travel-mlm-companies-guide

### DreamTrips (formerly WorldVentures)
- **Positioning:** members-only vacation club with curated trips.
- **History:** WorldVentures filed Chapter 11 in Dec 2020; revenue fell from $335M (2019) to $4M (2022); relaunched as DreamTrips. 14.6% of reps earned any commission. Sources: https://flawlessmlm.com/en/blog/travel-mlm-companies-guide , https://hostagencyreviews.com/blog/travel-mlms
- **Hook:** "Live more, travel more" style lifestyle selling (GUESS on current wording).

### TraVerus Global / Xstream Travel (formerly Paycation)
- **Positioning:** build a home-based travel business with tools, training and support. Xstream is the host agency; TraVerus is the MLM side. Source: https://behindmlm.com/companies/paycation-review-another-traverus-mlm-reboot/
- **Features:** agent tiers with 65% to 75% commission splits (older data), personal website, travel club membership required to stay active.
- **Hook (historic brand name):** "Paycation," get paid to vacation. Note: that is exactly the lifestyle claim the FTC warns about.

### MWR Life
- **Positioning:** travel and lifestyle savings membership. 86.22% of members in the lowest tier averaged $348/year; faced a 2022 regulatory inquiry over earnings claims (per one source). https://hostagencyreviews.com/blog/travel-mlms

### Note on "Global Travel Alliance"
Searches found no current travel MLM by that name. Skip it.

### Individual rep sites seen
- Catina (InteleTravel agent): "Becoming a Travel Agent from Home," lists training, flexibility, exclusive deals, commission and perks. https://www.catinathebroker.com/blog/travelagentwithinteletravel
- The Walking Mermaid: "How to Become A Travel Agent." https://thewalkingmermaid.com/blog/how-to-become-a-travel-agent
- Pattern: rep sites are mostly recruiting blogs. **Very few are built to win vacation buyers.** That is Jessica's opening.

## Top 10 headlines that work in this space
Built from the patterns above, rewritten to stay honest and FTC-safe.
1. Stop spending your vacation planning your vacation.
2. Plan less. Travel more.
3. Your personal travel advisor. Same price, one person to call.
4. Booking a cruise? Get a free second opinion before you click buy.
5. Tell me your dream trip. I'll send three options this week.
6. Love planning trips? Learn how travel advisors book them from home.
7. A home-based travel business, explained honestly. Real costs, no hype.
8. Already the friend everyone asks for travel tips?
9. Learn the travel business with a team that trains you, not pressures you.
10. Disney, cruises, all-inclusives: planned for you, start to finish.

## Top features to show on her site
1. Her real face, name, story and phone number (the one thing big competitors can't copy).
2. "Free trip quote" form or dream-trip quiz.
3. Trip types she books: cruises, all-inclusive, Disney, honeymoons, groups.
4. Supplier logos or "110+ travel suppliers" (verify she is allowed to use logos).
5. "How I'm paid" one-liner: suppliers pay me, you pay the same price (verify).
6. Real reviews and client trip photos.
7. Price-drop watch and perks (only if she actually does this).
8. Separate "Become a travel advisor" page with exact costs, training, who it's for, and the income disclosure link.
9. Book-a-coffee-chat button instead of a sign-up link.
10. Compliance footer: results vary, link to PlanNet Income Disclosure.

## What to avoid (competitors do it, it hurts trust)
- "Get paid to travel," "Paycation," lifestyle photos of cars and yachts.
- "Up to 70% off," "wholesale prices" without proof.
- Leading with recruiting on the same page as booking.
