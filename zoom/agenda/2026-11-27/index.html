Postcards: A Doodle Front And A Split Test

Daegan made a doodle version of each of our three postcards.

The back stays the same. The front is a picture instead of the headline, one you have to figure out, so you pick it up and turn it over.

Card 1: "Did you find him yet?" A crowd, and somewhere in it a little robot carrying someone off.

Card 2: "But... how can sand fall up?" An hourglass with the sand going up.

Card 3: "What is that?" A tiny devil under a magnifying glass.

Each card now has two QR codes, one for the headline front and one for the doodle front, so we can actually split test.

Send half with the doodle and half with the regular title, and see if we get a difference in response.

The mockup is open to anyone with the link, and the cards and all six QR codes are on the mail page too.

To decide together: we mail half of each card with each front. See them on the mail page. The mockup is here.

Knowing Which Card Brought Them In

When someone scans a postcard code, the site now remembers which card and which front they came from.

When they book and land on the page after the booking ("You're booked"), that counts as a booking for that card and that front. The night's sign-up counts the same way.

The numbers show on the scoreboard: one row per card, doodle front against headline front.

Claude tested a scan and then a booking, and both counted.

Next, we need a metrics database, so we can see everything in one place: how the Facebook ads are doing, the leads Jimmy gets from the groups, the postcards, and what comes from the lead portal. Daegan is starting on it now.

To decide together: how Jimmy's group leads get tagged, so they show up there too. See the scoreboard here.

The Free 15 Page Now Matches The Home Page Up Top

Daegan saw the free 15 page (the one our Facebook ads and the postcards send people to) was still on an older version.
