What Comes After The Free Fix, And What I Found Last Night

I spent Wednesday studying how other people sell AI help to small businesses.

The one I kept seeing is a report: about $999, delivered in 48 hours, with "5 hours a week back or a refund."

Then I ran my own irresistible offer process on ours, the direct response way: stack up everything they could want.

It didn't fit the person we're selling to. The drawing and the passage say why.

The Offer That Works On A Tired Owner

A direct response offer is built for someone who showed up hungry. He's in the room, he's leaning in, and a stack of everything he could want makes him say yes.

A local owner at the end of his day is not that person. He's tired and a little fuzzy. He's asking one small question: will this help my business? Pile a huge promise on him and he doesn't get excited. He gets tired, and he expects to watch you fail to deliver it.

Nobody goes looking for the guy who says "we could do that for you." What everybody hopes to find is the person whose answer goes past what they knew to ask.

So he asks for one thing. You show him everything that would work. You tell him which few you'd use. Then you say what a friend would say: I could do better than all of these.

His reaction is the whole sale: "That's better than I could have asked you for."

In the regular human world, that is the irresistible offer.

What that means for us: he asks for one thing. We show him everything that would work, say 15 ways to do it. We tell him the few we'd use, and why those beat the rest. Then he picks.

We're his guide, his expert and his friend. The friend's line, "I could do better than all of these," is what leads from tools that already exist to us building something for him.

The same shape runs all the way through. Free: he picks the job. Paid: we show him everything we found and the few we'd use, and he picks. Monthly: he picks the next one. He's the boss every time.

One thing I settled: the paid step is a meeting, not an assessment.

Not settled yet: the price of that meeting, and what the monthly looks like.

The menu and the "what does it cost?" answer still say "The Assessment, $1,000." I haven't changed them.

To decide together: is this how you already talk to owners, and does it fit what we can deliver? See the write-up here.
